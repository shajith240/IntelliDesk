The product

What problem does IntelliDesk solve, in one paragraph?Basic

Small teams answer customers from a shared support@ inbox. Questions get missed, two people answer the same email, nobody knows what's overdue, and the same routine questions are answered by hand every day. IntelliDesk connects that inbox, turns each email into a tracked ticket, classifies and prioritizes it with AI, drafts answers from the company's own knowledge base, lets a lead assign work to the right person, and tracks response deadlines — while keeping people in control of what is sent.

What are the product's differentiators?Intermediate
  • Bring your own AI key: the company pays Google directly for AI — cents per thousand emails — instead of per-answer AI fees.
  • Safe by default: drafts only until the admin opts in; strict rules for automatic answers; mail-loop protection; everything AI-generated is labelled.
  • Admin-led: the lead decides who works on what; agents see only their tickets.
  • White-label ready: one isolated workspace per company, replies sent from the company's own mailbox under its own name.
  • Works with Gmail today: a guided connection in a few minutes.

What does the AI actually cost a customer?Intermediate

At roughly 1,500 input and 400 output tokens per email plus one embedding, 1,000 emails use about 1.5 M input and 0.4 M output tokens: about $0.30 on Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite prices and about $1.45 on Gemini 2.5 Flash prices. Adding the reply-drafting call, a small team stays well under $5 a month. That's the core pitch against per-answer AI pricing.

Going to market

How could the first customer be won without a marketing budget?Intermediate
  1. Pick one niche with a shared Gmail support inbox and repetitive questions: D2C/Shopify brands, coaching institutes and ed-tech, clinics, travel agencies.
  2. Build trust assets: a demo workspace with realistic tickets, a 3-minute screen recording, a one-page site with the price, a privacy policy, a data processing agreement and a status page.
  3. Warm network first: alumni, professors running startups, the college incubator, family businesses — warm introductions convert far better than cold messages.
  4. Then 50 targets: companies with a support@ address; people complaining about helpdesk prices in reviews; relevant Reddit and LinkedIn communities. About 10 personalised messages a day.
  5. Discovery call: ask about volume, who answers, what's asked most, what happens at night — then show their kind of email flowing through the demo.
  6. 14-day pilot on their real inbox with automatic sending off; load their top 20 FAQs yourself.
  7. Close with numbers from the pilot (emails handled, drafts produced, first-reply time), founding price for annual prepay.
  8. Case study and referrals, then repeat in the same niche.

What is the project's actual primary goal?Basic

It is first a college project meant to show real engineering to interviewers: system design, applied database fundamentals, security and reliability — built like a product rather than a toy. Commercialising it is a possible later step, which is why the design already supports multiple isolated workspaces and customer-paid AI.
